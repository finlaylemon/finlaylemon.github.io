Lunar Logistics Rover

June 2026 – present

SolidWorks, SolidWorks Simulation

Lunar Logistics Rover, SolidWorks model. Drag to rotate.

An independent concept design for a crewed lunar south-pole utility rover, developed as a response to the Canadian Space Agency's Lunar Utility Vehicle (LUV) program. The rover is sized for 3,500 kg of payload including two crew, with remote and autonomous waypoint modes, and stows inside a 5.75 m lander fairing.

  • Requirements and systems engineering. Built the design around a decision register that traces requirements from CSA and NASA sources into design decisions, maintained interfaces between subsystems as the design changed, and tracked where the design does not yet comply.
  • Terramechanics. Applied the Bekker model with NASA regolith data to derive rolling resistance, traction limits on slopes, and the grouser height needed to climb them.
  • Drivetrain sizing. Converted the mobility results into wheel torque and speed requirements, then selected a frameless motor and 120:1 harmonic gearbox, checking continuous, peak and speed margins against catalogue limits.
  • Thermal analysis. Analysed cold and hot cases for a pumped fluid loop fed by MMRTG waste heat, including end-of-life RTG output, night survival and radiator sizing.
  • FEA and design iteration. Designed a new large-diameter wheel with titanium flexures, an aluminium spine and replaceable grousers, and iterated the flexure in SolidWorks Simulation from a failing first design to a factor of safety of 1.68 at the worst-case load.
  • Technical writing. Documented the analysis, trade-offs and open non-compliances in a 34-page technical report.

Mars Sample Retrieval Rover

Jan – Apr 2026

Course design project, team of three · SolidWorks, ROS 2, Nav2, Gazebo, RViz, Python

A course design project, in a team of three, for a rover to collect the sample tubes cached at Mars's Three Forks Depot, built on the CSA Small Planetary Rover Platform (SPRP).

  • Mechanism design and CAD. Led the rover design and produced all of the CAD, including the sample retrieval system: a robotic arm with a claw and camera, a ten-tube sample bank, and deployable solar panels.
  • Mission concept. Worked through surface operations, fault handling and COSPAR planetary protection requirements as part of the design report.
  • Robotics integration. Worked on point-to-point navigation for the hardware phase: ROS 2 and Nav2 on a Raspberry Pi 4 with Arduino motor control, LiDAR SLAM for room mapping, and testing in Gazebo before running on the rover.

Desktop Wind Tunnels

Oct 2025 – 2026

Independent project · SolidWorks, FDM 3D printing, COTS integration

Two low-cost desktop wind tunnels, designed as an alternative to the large commercial units used at Queen's. The open-return tunnel was built and tested; the closed-loop tunnel was taken through full CAD.

  • Design for additive manufacturing. Split the open-return tunnel into modular sections that print in about 20 hours on household filament, so any damaged part can be reprinted and replaced on its own.
  • Joint design. Designed a ridge-and-collar joint: printed collars snap over triangular ridges on each section and pull them tight, sealing the joint with a single bolt.
  • Flow conditioning and test. Designed the contraction, honeycomb and screens, diffuser and fan module, and confirmed flow quality with smoke visualisation over a test airfoil.

I then scaled the design up to a closed-loop tunnel twice the length, with a 5.29 contraction ratio, hexagonal honeycomb and turning vanes in each corner, for a theoretical 20 m/s. It was planned as a platform for comparison testing against an AGARD-B benchmark model.
